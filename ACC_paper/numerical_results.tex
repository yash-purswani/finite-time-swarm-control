All quantities are normalized. This report documents the rotating and compressing
multi-agent star formation tracking scenario with transient collision avoidance,
retaining the arbitrary formation demonstration (Exp.~1), the finite-time convergence
and coupling gain evaluation under rotating star formation (Exp.~2), the certified
safe versus unsafe transient comparison across a $2 \times 3$ grid (Exp.~3), and the
Experiment 4 coupling--compression rate parameter sweep.

\section{Model and parameters}
The physical swarm consists of $n = 10$ agents in $\mathbb{R}^2$ governed by the closed-loop velocity command:
\[
\dot x_i = u_i = \dot q_i - \delta_i - \operatorname{sig}^{\beta}(\delta_i)
- \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i(t)} w_{ij} M (\delta_i - \delta_j), \qquad \delta_i = x_i - q_i,
\]
where $\operatorname{sig}^{\beta}(z) = \operatorname{sign}(z)|z|^{\beta}$, $M = m I_2$, $\beta = 0.5$, $d_{\rm safe} = 1.0$~m, and $R_c = 6.0$~m.
The state-dependent interaction weights are
\[
w_{ij}(x, t) = \frac{a_{ij}(t)}{\|x_i - x_j\| + 0.2}, \qquad
a_{ij}(t) = \begin{cases} 1 & \|x_i - x_j\| < R_c \ (i \neq j), \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases}
\]
giving lower bound $\underline{w} = 1 / (R_c + 0.2) = 1/6.8$ and algebraic connectivity $\lambda_2(L_{\mathcal{H}}) = 10.0$.

The rotating and compressing target stations $q_i(t)$ follow:
\begin{align*}
r(t) &= \begin{bmatrix} t \\ 2 \sin(0.5 t) \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\dot r(t) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \cos(0.5 t) \end{bmatrix}, \\
q_i(t) &= r(t) + a(t) R(\omega t) p_i^0, \qquad
\dot q_i(t) = \dot r(t) + \dot a(t) R(\omega t) p_i^0 + a(t) \dot R(\omega t) p_i^0,
\end{align*}
where $p_i^0 = b_i [\cos\phi_i, \sin\phi_i]^T$, $\phi_i = \pi/2 + \pi i/5$, and $b_i = 2.8$ for even $i$ and $1.22$ for odd $i$ ($i=0,\ldots,9$), centered such that $\sum_i p_i^0 = 0$. The baseline minimum pairwise distance is $DQ_0 \approx 1.4342$~m, and the template diameter is $\ell_{\mathcal{H},0} \approx 5.3259$~m.
Rotation operates at angular velocity $\omega = \pi/4$~rad/s ($45^\circ$/s) via
\[
R(\omega t) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\omega t) & -\sin(\omega t) \\ \sin(\omega t) & \cos(\omega t) \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\dot R(\omega t) = \omega \begin{bmatrix} -\sin(\omega t) & -\cos(\omega t) \\ \cos(\omega t) & -\sin(\omega t) \end{bmatrix}.
\]
Formation scale contraction follows
\[
a(t) = a_\infty + (a_0 - a_\infty)e^{-\kappa t}, \qquad a_0 = 1.10, \quad a_\infty = 0.75, \quad \dot a(t) = -\kappa(a_0 - a_\infty)e^{-\kappa t}.
\]
Notice that for all $t \ge 0$, the station plan satisfies $d_q(t) \ge a_\infty DQ_0 = 1.0757\text{ m} > d_{\rm safe} = 1.0\text{ m}$ and $\ell_q(t) \le a_0 \ell_{\mathcal{H},0} = 5.8585\text{ m} < R_c = 6.0\text{ m}$. The assigned stations are strictly safe and connected for all time.

The initial perturbation $\delta_i(0)$ is zero-mean ($\sum_i \delta_i(0)=0$), generated via random annular ring perturbation around each target station $q_i(0)$ with radius $r_i \in [0.3, 0.7]$~m ($d_{\rm safe} = 1.0$~m, $R_c = 6.0$~m, seed 123). At $t=0$, physical separation is safe ($d_x(0) = 1.0864\text{ m} > d_{\rm safe}$) and connected ($\ell_x(0) = 5.7419\text{ m} < R_c$), with initial error norm $\|\delta_0\| \approx 1.6532$, initial disagreement distortion $\rho_0 = \sqrt{2}\|\eta_0\| \approx 2.3380$~m, and universal settling deadline $\overline T = 2\ln(1+\sqrt{\|\delta_0\|}) \approx 1.6534$~s.

The geometric clearance margin is $s(t) = \min\{d_q(t) - d_{\rm safe},\; R_c - \ell_{\mathcal{H}}(t)\} = \min\{a(t)DQ_0 - d_{\rm safe},\; R_c - a(t)\ell_{\mathcal{H},0}\} > 0$. For coupling gain $m$, the effective decay rate is $\gamma = 1 + m \underline{w}\lambda_2(L_{\mathcal{H}}) = 1 + m \frac{10}{6.8}$, and the required rate is
\[
\gamma_{\rm req}(\kappa) = \sup_{\tau \in (0, \overline T]} \frac{\ln(\rho_0 / s(\tau))}{\tau}.
\]
The dynamic disagreement distortion tube is bounded by
\[
\rho_{\mathcal{H}}(t) = \min\{\sqrt{2}R(t),\; \rho_0 e^{-\gamma t}\}, \qquad
R(t) = \max\left\{0, \left((1+\sqrt{\|\delta_0\|})e^{-\frac{1}{2} t}-1\right)^2\right\},
\]
where $R(t)$ is the pure Theorem~1 station tracking bound (independent of graph and coupling gain $m$).

\section{Finite-time tracking and coupling gain evaluation}
Experiment~2 evaluates the rotating-compressing star formation maneuver ($\kappa=2.0$) across coupling gains $m \in \{0, 0.5, 2, 2.5, 5, 10\}$. Figure~\ref{fig:exp2_errors} depicts station tracking error $\|\delta(t)\|_2$, formation disagreement $\|\eta(t)\|_2$, and the initial control command $\|u(t)\|_2$. In panels (a) and (b), error curves are cut at $10^{-4}$ to present the clean finite-time transient down to exact settling.

Increasing the coupling gain $m$ accelerates disagreement decay, reducing the settling time to the $10^{-6}$ error threshold from $1.1350$~s at $m=0$ down to $0.2250$~s at $m=10$ (Table~\ref{tab:star_gain}). All runs settle well within the theoretical universal deadline $\overline T = 1.6534$~s.

\section{Star formation safety and connectivity transient}
Experiment~3 analyzes the fast compression maneuver ($\kappa = 6.0$) over $t \in [0, 2.5]$~s, comparing two regimes in a $2 \times 3$ grid format (Figure~\ref{fig:star_3x3}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Row 1 (Safe Case, $m = 2.5$):} With coupling gain $m = 2.5$, the convergence rate is $\gamma = 4.6765$. In simulation, the swarm settles within $t_{\rm set} = 0.4600$~s. Both minimum pairwise separation ($d_{x,\min} = 1.0756\text{ m} > d_{\rm safe} = 1.0\text{ m}$) and maximum diameter ($\ell_{x,\max} = 5.7419\text{ m} < R_c = 6.0\text{ m}$) are strictly preserved across the entire transient without violation.
\item \textbf{Row 2 (Unsafe Mid-Transient Collision, $m = 0.0$):} With uncoupled agents ($m = 0.0$), the decay rate is only $\gamma = 1.0000$. Although stations remain strictly safe ($d_q(t) \ge 1.0757$~m), rapid maneuver compression outpaces uncoupled error convergence. As a result, the uncoupled swarm suffers a genuine physical collision mid-transient: $d_x(t) < d_{\rm safe}$ for $t \in [0.11, 0.50]$~s (duration $0.39$~s), reaching a minimum distance of $d_{x,\min} = 0.9548$~m (a collision breach of $4.52$~cm). Network connectivity is maintained ($\ell_{x,\max} = 5.7419\text{ m} < R_c$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Coupling--speed parameter map}
Experiment~4 maps the parameter space over a $4 \times 4$ grid: coupling gain $m \in \{0.0, 1.5, 3.0, 5.0\}$ and maneuver compression rate $\kappa \in \{2.0, 4.0, 6.0, 8.0\}$. For each parameter pair, the sufficient condition $1 + m \underline{w}\lambda_2(L_{\mathcal{H}}) > \gamma_{\rm req}(\kappa)$ is evaluated against simulated physical separation and connectivity.

The grid yields $\MapBCount$ uncertified but observed safe configurations (Category~B) and $\MapCCount$ observed collision cases (Category~C at $m=0.0, \kappa \ge 6.0$, where minimum distance drops to $\MapMinSeparation$~m $< 1.0$~m). All coupled configurations ($m \ge 1.5$) preserve both safety ($d_{x,\min} \ge 1.0756$~m) and connectivity ($\ell_{x,\max} = 5.7419$~m) across all compression speeds without any validation failure. Peak control effort spans $\MapMinPeak$--$\MapMaxPeak$.

Figure~\ref{fig:exp4_map} (left) overlays the continuous sufficient gain boundary $m_{\rm req}(\kappa) = \max\left(0, \frac{\gamma_{\rm req}(\kappa) - 1}{\underline{w}\lambda_2(L_{\mathcal{H}})}\right)$ across $\kappa \in [2.0, 8.0]$. While uncoupled agents ($m=0$) collide under aggressive compression ($\kappa \ge 6.0$), introducing positive coupling gain $m \ge 1.5$ completely eliminates transient collisions. Figure~\ref{fig:exp4_map} (right) presents the corresponding peak control command map.
