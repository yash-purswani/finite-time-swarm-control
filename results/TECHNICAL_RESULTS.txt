Numerical interpretation and limitations
All apparent envelope excesses are saved, including near-zero solver residuals.
The acceptance budget is an absolute 1e-8 norm error; retry integration uses
tenfold tighter tolerances and half the maximum step, never a larger budget.
Observed distance extrema use dense scanning, crossing events, and pair-specific
local optimization. They do not establish rigorous all-time observed safety; the
analytic tube certificate does. Planned feasibility is checked beyond the horizon.
Settling is the final crossing of an explicit tolerance over a finite horizon,
checked at output samples and adaptive nodes, not proof of exact floating-point zero.
Use 1e-6 for quantitative comparisons; 1e-8 is diagnostic if solver-sensitive.
Physical
relative tolerance scales with absolute x, whereas the independent error solver
scales with delta; the equivalence diagnostic therefore uses tighter tolerances.
Radau is checked on a short high-gain diagnostic before zero singularities.
Full nominal trajectories use DOP853; nonsmooth near-zero step reduction remains
expensive and is not treated by changing the controller. Energy uses numerical
quadrature; peaks are evaluated at saved dense-output samples. No actuator cap
or physical robot dimensions are included. No Monte Carlo experiments were run.

Discrepancies found in the original implementation
1. Signed power was applied to the centroid error instead of each delta_i.
2. Coupling was divided by n, absent from the supplied report's controller.
3. The old integrator latched/projected centroid components at a threshold.
4. Old theoretical helpers used exponential station convergence and separate
   centroid/ISS claims from an earlier mathematical draft.
5. Range-limited graph/backbone certificates and full physical command reporting
   were missing. The true-distance denominator and analytic quintic formation
   derivatives were reusable and retained.
The current implementation matches the supplied report; the report is untouched.
